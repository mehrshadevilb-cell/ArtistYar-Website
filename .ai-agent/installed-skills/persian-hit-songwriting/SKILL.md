# Persian Hit Songwriting Skill

## Quality gate
Before returning lyrics, inspect:
1. Hook/title clarity and repeatability.
2. Natural Persian prosody: stressed syllables must align with musical emphasis; never force syntax for rhyme.
3. Line-length consistency inside each section; prefer close syllable counts and natural breath points.
4. Rhyme quality: avoid self-rhyme, repeated end words, forced/archaic rhyme, and predictable rhyme pairs. Use slant/assonant rhyme when it sounds more natural.
5. Register consistency: colloquial, literary or deliberate mixed register.
6. POV and tense consistency.
7. Concrete imagery: replace generic emotion labels with observable objects/actions/details.
8. Verse/chorus contrast; V2 must develop the idea rather than paraphrase V1.
9. Anti-cliche: detect stock Persian phrases and replace them with situation-specific language.
10. Pronunciation risks: names, foreign words, ambiguous forms and difficult consonant clusters.
11. Singability: read every line aloud; flag mouthfuls and awkward stress.
12. Originality: learn only abstract patterns from public songs; never reproduce or closely transform copyrighted lyrics.

## Persian-specific prosody
Persian pop/folk lyrics can be analyzed as quantitative rhythmic patterns. Treat syllable length and natural word stress as first-class features. Colloquial Persian may use systematic vowel-shortening and should not be judged as broken classical meter merely because it differs from formal prosody.

## Hook test
A useful hook should be easy to say, emotionally legible, rhythmically compact and capable of surviving repetition. Test it in isolation, then against the verse immediately before it. The verse should create tension rather than reveal the chorus verbatim.

## Rhyme test
Normalize Arabic/Persian letter variants before comparison. Compare phonetic-ish endings, not raw spelling only. Penalize identical end words and accidental repetition. Prefer a strong vowel/consonant family over a forced perfect rhyme.

## Human tests
Run:
- first-listen: does something stick quickly?
- sing: can a singer say it naturally without rushing?
- memory: is there one signature phrase?
- emotion: is emotion carried by scenes/details, not only adjectives?
- conversation: does it sound like a real person?
- cliche: how much stock language remains?
- artist voice: does it preserve the user's vocabulary and register?

## Output behavior
Do not claim a song will become a hit. State concrete problems and provide concrete alternatives. Avoid generic advice such as "make it more emotional". Name the exact line/problem and the exact type of fix.

## Iterative refinement
Default to two passes:
Pass 1: tighten filler, repetition and weak rhyme.
Pass 2: strengthen specificity, hook, prosody and transitions.
Do not overwrite the user's original draft unless explicitly requested.

## Research corpus
When chart data is available, use only metadata and abstract pattern features: rank, plays, genre, mood, structure, hook behavior, rhyme family, repetition, register and emotional arc. Do not store or quote third-party lyric text.
